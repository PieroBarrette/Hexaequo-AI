HEXAEQUO

Hexaequo · Français

Rules

Hexaequo is played on a board that does not exist yet: the players build it as they go, one tile at a time.

Winning

You win the moment any one of these is true at the end of your turn:

  • you have captured six enemy disks;
  • you have captured three enemy rings;
  • your opponent has no piece left on the board — even if pieces remain in their reserve.

Material

Each player owns 9 tiles, 6 disks and 3 rings in their colour. Black moves first.

Setup

The two black tiles and the two white ones form a block of four. A disk sits on each of the two opposite tiles — the only pair that do not touch — on a tile of its own colour. Those pieces come from the reserves, so each player starts with 7 tiles and 5 disks.

The opening position. Black's disk is top right, White's is bottom left; the two tiles carrying them are the only ones in the block that are not neighbours.

Your turn

On your turn, exactly one action: lay a tile, place a piece, or move a piece. Passing is not allowed, and the action must change the position.

Laying a tile

Lay a tile from your reserve on any empty cell touching at least two tiles already on the board. Their colour does not matter, only how many.

The dashed cells each touch two existing tiles, so any of them may take a tile. The cell marked in red touches only one, so it may not.

Placing a piece

Place a disk or a ring from your reserve on an empty tile of your own colour. A ring has a price: hand back one enemy disk you have captured. It returns to your opponent's reserve and no longer counts towards your six.

Black may place on either empty black tile, never on a white one. The ring is only available once Black holds at least one captured white disk to give back.

Moving a disk

A disk takes one step onto an adjacent empty tile, or makes a chain of one or more jumps. A jump crosses one adjacent piece — friendly or enemy — and lands on the next tile in the same straight line, which must exist and be empty. An enemy piece crossed is captured at once; a friendly one is merely flown over. You may stop after any jump.

Black jumps the white disk and lands on the empty tile beyond it, capturing as it passes. A second jump is available from there, and Black may take it or stop.

Within a single chain, a disk may not land again on a cell it has already occupied since its last capture. Hopping back and forth over friendly pieces is therefore impossible, while a genuine capturing chain that loops back to its starting cell stays legal.

Moving a ring

A ring makes one leap, of exactly two tiles — twelve destinations on a full board. Nothing blocks it: the cell it passes over may hold any piece, or no tile at all. An enemy piece on the arrival tile is captured; a friendly one forbids the leap.

All twelve cells at distance two from the ring. It may leap onto any of them that carries a tile, capturing an enemy piece there.

Points that are easy to get wrong

  • Tile colour only restricts placement. A piece that moves may finish on any empty tile, whatever its colour.
  • Captured pieces leave the game, with one exception: a disk handed back to pay for a ring. Captured rings never come back.
  • A jump is not compulsory. You may play a quiet move even when a capture is on offer.
  • The board tops out at 18 tiles, nine from each player.

ExAequo

The game is a draw — ExAequo — if the player to move has no legal move at all, if the same position occurs three times, or if fifty moves pass for each player with no capture and nothing placed on the board.

Any capture, any tile laid and any piece placed set that count of fifty back to zero. Those are the moves a game cannot take back: the board only ever grows, and a captured piece only ever returns as the single disk handed over to pay for a ring. Once both players have stopped making them, nothing that happens can be undone or repeated into a win, and the game is level.
